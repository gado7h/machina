# Archived

After a lot of time spent on this project, I've decided to archive it.

Machina was my attempt at building an x86 emulator. I started it because I wanted to challenge myself and learn how emulators actually work under the hood. I knew it wouldn't be easy, but I also didn't realize just how massive x86 really is.

The further I got, the more I realized that every feature opened the door to ten more problems. Instruction decoding, registers, flags, memory, interrupts, protected mode, and countless edge cases—it quickly became a project that was much bigger than I could realistically finish.

There were definitely moments where I wanted to give up, but there were also plenty of moments where finally getting something to work felt incredibly rewarding.

In the end, I couldn't get the emulator to a point where I was happy with it, and forcing myself to keep working on it stopped being fun. Rather than let it sit around pretending it's still active, I'd rather archive it and move on to new projects.

Even though this project "failed" in terms of reaching its goal, I don't consider it a failure.

Machina taught me more than I expected. I came away with a much better understanding of CPU architecture, instruction execution, memory management, debugging low-level code, and just how much work goes into software that most people take for granted.

This repository is staying up because it represents that learning process. Every bug, failed experiment, and abandoned idea taught me something, and I think that's worth keeping around.

Maybe one day I'll come back and try again with more experience. Until then, Machina will stay here as a reminder that not every project needs to succeed to be valuable.

Thanks to everyone who took an interest in it.
